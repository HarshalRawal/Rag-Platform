## Health Department Actions in the First 24 Hours After a Cyclone RED Alert — SDMP §6.3.3

> **Contextual note on the warning system:** The SDMP follows the IMD four-stage cyclone warning framework. A **"Cyclone Warning"** (functionally equivalent to the RED/most-severe alert) is issued at least **24 hours before** expected landfall, updated every 3 hours with intensity, track, and storm surge data [SDMP §3.2.2]. The Health Department's SOP for this window is structured in four explicit time-bands.

---

### ⏱ Phase 1 — Immediate (0–2 Hours after Warning)

The moment a cyclone warning is received, the following is triggered simultaneously at the **state and district level** [SDMP §6.3.3, Table 6.2]:

- **State/District Disaster Response Steering Committee meeting** convened immediately.
- **State/District Technical Task Force meeting** held.
- **CDMO (Chief District Medical Officer)** convenes a meeting of all Disaster Committee members to discuss modalities for mass casualty management.
- The CDMO **assumes the role of Incident Commander** and coordinates all operations from the control room located at the ADMO (PH) or ADMO (Med) office.
- **ADMO (PH)** becomes second-in-command / Operations Chief; **ADMO (Med)** leads mass casualty response, supported by a team including Transportation In-Charge, Store In-Charge, Liaison Officer, Logistics Chief, and Planning & Coordination Chief.
- **One ambulance with a team** of Medical Officers, Pharmacist, Staff Nurse, and an attendant is **immediately dispatched** to the site of occurrence.
- A **Control Room is opened** in the ADMO (Med) office with telephone, computer, printer, and email for communication and coordination with Blood Bank, Radiology, and Pathology.
- **Inter-sectoral coordination established** immediately with Police, Fire Services, Red Cross, corporate and private hospitals, ambulance services, NGOs, Voluntary Organizations, Water, Electricity, Sanitation, Civil Defense, ESI, Railways, and Transport departments.

---

### ⏱ Phase 2 — Intermediate Response (2–6 Hours)

[SDMP §6.3.3, Table 6.2]

- **Full activation of the chain of command** — from state level down to village level.
- **Health Surveillance Systems control rooms activated** at State, District, and Block levels.
- **Rapid Resource Mapping** of available health assets conducted.
- **Sharing of information** with all concerned departments: Revenue & Disaster Management, Housing & Urban Development, PRI, School & Mass Education, Women & Child Development, Food & Supplies, Industries, Mines, Works, Transport, Fire, Police, RWSS, OSDMA, Civil Defence, Red Cross, UNDP, and Surface Transport.
- **Risk communication messages coordinated**, and networking with other hospitals, related departments, and health-related volunteer organisations initiated.
- **Population at risk identified**, including vulnerable sub-groups.

---

### ⏱ Phase 3 — Extended Intermediate Response (6–12 Hours)

[SDMP §6.3.3, Table 6.2]

- **Collection and analysis of information** from health surveillance control rooms, casualty systems, laboratory systems, and 108 ambulance service centres at Block/District/State/MCH level.
- **Activation of full clinical infrastructure:**
  - Operation Theatres (OT)
  - Labour Rooms (LR)
  - ICUs
  - Special Newborn Care Units (SNCU) and Neonatal Care Units (NCU)
  - X-Ray units
  - Blood Banks
  - Laboratories
  - At all levels: CHC / Sub-Divisional Hospital / District Headquarters Hospital / MCH / Accredited Private Hospitals
- **Executive briefings and shift-change information** prepared and updated.
- **Quick assessment of available resources** completed; proposals for supply of required items to affected areas submitted.

---

### ⏱ Phase 4 — Extended Response (12–24 Hours)

[SDMP §6.3.3, Table 6.2]

- **Address local emergency case management** — early referral, relocation of resources, and assessment of mental and behavioral health support needs.
- **Assess magnitude of impact** on affected population.
- **Prepare for state/national/international assistance** — formal proposals for specific need-based interventions prepared.
- **Planned distribution** of available resources and assistance received.
- **Roles and responsibilities of each department defined** in preparation for sustained operations.
- **Preparation for transition** to extended operations or response disengagement.

---

### 🏥 On-Site and Hospital-Level Actions (Concurrent Throughout the 24-Hour Window)

**On-Site:** [SDMP §6.3.3]
- Cases **triaged** by attending doctor team at the site.
- Minor injuries given first aid; prioritised cases given preliminary treatment and referred to nearby District Headquarters Hospital / secondary / tertiary care.
- If casualty load is overwhelming, a **Medical Relief Centre is opened** at a Government PHC/CHC/Private/School/Panchayat office.
- **Mobile Medical Units with Advanced Life Support** systems and doctor teams dispatched.
- Ambulances, 108 ambulances, and other transport deployed for triage-based referrals.
- Nearby CHC/SDH/DHH of the parent or neighbouring district supports triage and transport.

**At Hospitals:** [SDMP §6.3.3]
- Doctor on duty receives and re-triages all disaster-affected patients.
- OPD/IPD alerted; additional MOs pulled from other wards.
- **Separate wards/beds arranged** for casualty surge.
- **Patient Resuscitation Area** activated in casualty for Priority 1 (seriously injured) patients.
- **Reception area with registration facility and help desks** opened near the casualty.
- If mass casualties extend beyond 24 hours, MOs are deployed from other areas within and outside the district.
