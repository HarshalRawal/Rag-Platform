## ☠️ Official Death Toll from Cyclone Fani in Odisha

### Total Casualties

Two figures appear across official and UN sources, reflecting different reporting timelines:

| Source | Date | Reported Deaths |
|---|---|---|
| State Emergency Operation Centre (SEOC), Bhubaneswar | 9 May 2019 | **41 deaths** |
| UNICEF Situation Report | Post-event | **64 deaths** |

The **41-death figure** is the official Government of Odisha count as of 9 May 2019 (6 days after landfall), based on district-wise reporting to the SEOC. The **64-death figure** is cited in the UNICEF Fani SitRep as the final consolidated count from the State Emergency Operation Centre, reflecting updated tallies after field verification.

### 🏆 District with the Highest Casualties: **Puri**

The district-wise breakdown as of 9 May 2019 (from the SEOC Situation Report) is:

| District | Deaths Reported |
|---|---|
| **Puri** | **21** |
| Cuttack | 5 |
| Mayurbhanj | 4 |
| Jajpur | 4 |
| Khordha | 4 |
| Kendrapara | 3 |
| **Total** | **41** |

**Puri recorded the highest casualties (21 deaths — over half the total)**, which is consistent with it being the landfall district, where Cyclone Fani crossed the coast at 8:12 AM on 3 May 2019 with maximum sustained wind speeds of **175–185 km/h gusting to 210 km/h**. All telephone and mobile connectivity in Puri district was completely down in the immediate aftermath. [Fani_JRNA §1 Background; §2 Extent of Damages]

### 🌀 Context: The Importance of These Numbers

The relatively low death toll — despite Fani being a **Category 4 cyclone** affecting over **15 million people** — was the result of an unprecedented mass evacuation of **1.47 million people** before landfall, mobilising 45,000 volunteers, 2,000 emergency workers, and 1,00,000 officials. The UN recognised this as a **global good practice** for effective preparedness. By comparison, the 1999 Odisha Super Cyclone claimed approximately **10,000 lives**.
